The boat taught me before the machines did
A short note on instruments, judgement, and the skills worth keeping alive even when a faster path exists.
 

There is a particular kind of loss that never announces itself. You do not wake up one morning unable to navigate without a map on your phone, or unable to sit with a hard problem long enough for an answer to surface. It happens the way erosion happens — one reasonable convenience at a time.

This issue is about the discipline that resists it: choosing, on purpose, which capabilities to keep close. Not a rejection of speed — a refusal to drift into it.
Stay able. Choose your conveniences. Do not drift.
