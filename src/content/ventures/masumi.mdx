<section class="vp-section">
  <h2 class="serif vp-h2">What it is</h2>
  <div class="vp-prose">
    <p>AI agents are starting to do real work — and increasingly, work for each other. But an agent on
      its own has no passport, no bank account, and no paper trail. Masumi supplies all three: a
      decentralised identity and registry so you know which agent you are dealing with, escrowed
      payments so agents can buy and sell services, and on-chain records of every transaction.</p>
    <p>The idea came from building agents at Plan.Net and seeing the gap. I took it to Patrick Tobler
      and the team at NMKR, and together with Serviceplan Group we launched Masumi in November 2024.</p>
  </div>
</section>

<section class="vp-section">
  <h2 class="serif vp-h2">Why it matters</h2>
  <div class="vp-prose">
    <p>Every economy runs on boring infrastructure: identity, payment, contracts, records. Nobody writes
      keynotes about them, and nothing works without them. If agents are going to transact at scale, the
      question of accountability cannot be answered after the fact by a platform's goodwill. It has to be
      answerable by the system itself.</p>
    <p>That is why Masumi is an open protocol rather than a walled garden — the trust layer should not
      belong to whoever happens to own the biggest marketplace.</p>
  </div>
</section>

<section class="vp-section">
  <h2 class="serif vp-h2">How it holds to the principle</h2>
  <div class="vp-prose">
    <ul class="vp-principles">
      <li><span class="vp-pk">Verifiable identity</span> Agents are registered and recognisable, so
        reputation can accrue to the right actor.</li>
      <li><span class="vp-pk">Payment with escrow</span> Money moves when work is delivered — not on a
        promise.</li>
      <li><span class="vp-pk">An audit trail by default</span> Decisions and transactions leave a record
        anyone entitled to can inspect.</li>
    </ul>
  </div>
